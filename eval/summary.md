**Update, Nov 13: the same models without our fine-tune (zero-shot).** Neither base model turns the render into a real person by itself. The judge rated them 2.1 to 2.4 out of 10 for realism on held-out people, about the same as the plain render (1.5 to 2.3), against 7.7 to 8.1 after fine-tuning. Their lip sync looks high (0.90 to 0.94) only because they mostly hand the render back unchanged. Pushing base SD-Turbo harder (strength 0.75) makes a more photographic picture (3.1) of a different person and loses the lips (0.14). Speed is the same with or without the fine-tune. The table and four-panel videos are in the next section; every video on the page now carries its original voice track.

**Result: it works at low resolution, and the fast version already runs faster than real time.** A one-step image model (SD-Turbo + LoRA, 384×384) turns the stylised 3D render into a photoreal person who keeps the render's pose, blinks, mouth shapes and timing. On 12 held-out clips of 8 people it never saw, a blind Claude judge preferred it over the plain render in 24 of 24 trials (realism 8.0 vs 1.5 out of 10; real footage scores 8.9 in the three-way test, the model 6.4). It measured 21 ms per frame (47 fps) on a rented RTX 5090.

- **Lip sync and motion are preserved by the fast model:** mouth-opening correlation with the input render 0.92 (the real video itself scores 0.93 against the render), 0 ms lag, blink agreement F1 0.88, head pose within 0.5°.
- **The Wan 2.1 VACE video model (LoRA fine-tuned) looks at least as real and holds identity steadier, but loses the lips:** mouth correlation 0.55, blink F1 0.59, and it runs at 6.9 fps with a 6-step distilled sampler. It also only follows the render's hair and clothes when given a text prompt.
- **On our own avatar renders:** Juno becomes a convincing real woman in the same cardigan and pose (fast model, lip correlation 0.84–0.89 on the two sharp clips). Muse does not work with the fast model (half plush, half human); prompted Wan turns Muse into a real-looking plush bear, but a different bear that barely follows the motion.
- **Main weaknesses:** identity is "a plausible person", not a fixed one (33 training identities; within-clip identity stability 0.86 vs 0.95 for real video); hair and edges shimmer (frame-to-frame change 1.21× real, 0.97× with a free latent blend); the training renders are a different style from the live renderer.
- **Spend:** $4.57 of GPU rental (RunPod) against the $20 budget. All text and judge calls ran on the Claude subscription. All rented GPUs are terminated.
