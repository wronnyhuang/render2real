- **Feeding the model its own previous frame.** The temporal variant learned to copy the previous frame; fed its own output it collapses into noise within about 10 frames. Fixing it needs training on its own rollouts. Not attempted.
- **The small U-Net's picture quality.** Blurry; three-way realism 3.3 vs 6.4 for the turbo model, and it falls apart on Muse/Juno. Its GAN-loss version diverged.
- **Wan loses lip sync.** Mouth correlation 0.55 with the prompt, 0.52 without, 0.07 on the 5 clips sampled at 20 steps. It runs at 16 fps natively, and the video prior re-imagines mouth motion instead of copying it. Zero-shot Wan (no fine-tune) kept the pose but stayed cartoon-like (judge realism 3 on the one clip run).
- **Wan without a prompt ignores the look.** A grey-haired man in a navy shirt came out as a brown-haired woman.
- **Muse.** There are no photoreal plush toys in the training data. The fast model produces an uncanny plush-human mix; Wan produces a different teddy bear. A photoreal Muse needs its own target data (for example a physical plush filmed, or generated reference footage).
- **Renderer mismatch.** Training renders come from the Blender de-render (toy-like heads, helmet hair, flat grey studio). The live renderer looks different, so lip correlation on drive clips drops to about 0.5 on average. Two of the four Juno clips are soft upscales of a 720p session recording.
- **Identity diversity.** The local Seamless copy has video for only 50 participants, not the ~121 first counted; 41 were usable. Women drift toward similar faces, and train clips come out nearly memorised.
- **Two Claude interruptions mid-run** (a quota limit at about 12:58 PM and a revoked login at about 3:13 PM) left rented GPUs idle for roughly 50 minutes in total, about $1.20 of the spend. The Wan agent did not write its own report; its numbers here come from its saved files. A 20-step prompted Wan run may have finished on the pod and was not retrieved before the pod was terminated.
